### Royal Daring Hockey Club — communication and sponsoring
Brussels, Belgium · founded 1922 · season 2025-26
Outcome: bilingual FR/NL website and around 30 visuals delivered

Turned a century-old amateur hockey club's fragmented communication into a sponsor-ready
acquisition system.

- **Context** — 19 teams from youth to senior squads, around 620 members, strong local
  footprint, promotion to Division Honneur on the horizon.
- **Problem** — How can an amateur club turn scattered communication assets into a
  coherent, credible, sponsor-ready identity capable of supporting partner acquisition
  and long-term development?
- **Situation on arrival** — No structured visual identity, no sponsorship material
  beyond a static three-tier PDF, no institutional LinkedIn presence, no reusable
  production workflow.
- **Delivered** — Live bilingual FR/NL sponsor website; complete brand guidelines and a
  reusable template system across four content formats (matchday, institutional, event,
  recruitment); a nine-page PDF brochure generated programmatically in Python so the club
  can update figures without desktop publishing software; reusable social, signage and
  editorial templates; an editorial handover workspace.
- **Sponsor offer** — Rebuilt from a static three-tier PDF into a five-tier package plus
  a Youth Pack, each with explicit visibility formats: field assets, jersey placements,
  LED board, match posters, social placements, CSR and youth programme framing, business
  club access. Twenty-two existing partner logos integrated as social proof. Created the
  club's LinkedIn page and professional positioning for partner outreach.
- **Handover** — Playbook for the incoming intern, editorial charter comparing the
  starting point to a prescriptive target, eleven ready-to-use post templates with brief
  and caption, live task tracker, exported in a format any assistant can reuse.
- **Role** — Product direction, brand system, content architecture, AI-assisted
  implementation, visual QA, deployment.
- **Live site** — https://leafy-pavlova-69305b.netlify.app

### AS Nortkerque 95 — head of communications
Nortkerque, France · football club founded 1995 · January → June 2025
Outcome: +467% sponsor click-through rate, over one million views

Rebuilt a local club's communication from nothing.

- **Season results** — 1M+ views, +93.8% interactions (24,200), 44,000 accounts reached
  (+24%), +28.2% follower growth, +467% sponsor click-through rate, across Instagram and
  Facebook.
- **Starting point** — No content calendar, no visual guidelines, no recurring formats.
  Sponsors had no visibility beyond pitch-side banners. The club had a community but no
  voice.
- **Sponsor activation** — "Les Pronos du Sultan", a weekly prediction game with local
  partner Sultan Kebab. Deliberately low-tech: an online form, a manual leaderboard, a
  free meal for the top three. It drove +467% click-through and weekly returning
  engagement. A second version, ASN95 Predict, is in beta: live rankings, user profiles
  and a sponsor-ready data capture layer to make the activation repeatable for any
  future partner.
- **Content and identity** — Match posters, result graphics and a weekly interview
  series. Over fifteen visuals per season across three recurring formats, with five or
  more sponsors integrated per visual. The visual language was built week by week until
  the templates were locked mid-season.
- **Photography** — 33 events covered, 500+ shots per match, around 50 delivered per
  session, 1,650+ edited photos. Coverage included 26 league matches, four youth training
  camps, two general assemblies and the club's 30th anniversary. Separate youth academy
  photoshoot with 50+ children in branded kits for sponsor content.
- **Lessons drawn** — Consistency beats any single viral post; sponsors need stories
  rather than banner placement; prove the concept before scaling it.

### BLAST.tv — strategic audit
2025 · academic case study · 23-page dossier

An audit of esports tournament organiser BLAST facing EFG, its Saudi PIF-backed
competitor, with a market-entry recommendation and a three-phase roadmap.

- **Situation** — BLAST holds around $11M in cash against EFG's $1.5Bn valuation, making
  direct Western confrontation unwinnable. FY24 revenue $82.1M (+13%), net result −$3.96M,
  gross margin 15.7%. Valve's 2025 ban on franchised partner leagues removes BLAST's main
  competitive barrier.
- **Recommendation** — Move from a boutique model to mass premium by entering India
  through a joint venture with Reliance: a mobile-first ecosystem addressing 600 million
  gamers, a market growing at 19% CAGR toward $9.2Bn by 2029, with distribution through
  JioGames and a cost structure well below European production.
- **Roadmap** — Phase one (H1 2026): India launch with a Mumbai production hub, Jio and
  Paytm integration, a mobile test event. Phase two (H2 2026): monetisation through a
  fantasy platform and watch-to-earn rewards, targeting +50% ARPU. Phase three (2027):
  break-even with a revenue mix of 60% Europe, 20% India, 20% MENA.
- **Differentiation** — Position BLAST as the reputationally safe option for Western
  sponsors subject to CSRD reporting, in contrast with EFG's ownership structure.
- **Frameworks** — PESTEL, VRIO and SWOT. Extends the M1 thesis on LEC/GRP versus CS2
  economic models, which included an interview with BLAST VP James Woollard.
